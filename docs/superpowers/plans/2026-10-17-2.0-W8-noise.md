# 2.0 W8: noise and hardware models — implementation plan

**Goal:** the noise, hardware and simulation items the inventory lists for W8 (spec W8: D5, D6,
Q13, X1–X3, and PECOS's density- and process-matrix simulators, U9's remainder), each checked
against a reference.

**Where things stand.**
- D5 and D6 are done (W7): Deltakit ported whole, with its noise models (SD6, SI1000, toy,
  phenomenological, T1/T2-derived physical noise, per-gate profiles, `Leakage`/`Relax`) and its
  QPU model (native gate sets with times, compilation to CZ/CZSWAP, parallelisation, layer
  merging). Its circuits and models equal Deltakit's own.
- Q13 is done (W6): qLDPC's `NoiseModel` rules, depolarizing, SI1000, Pauli channels and
  `as_noiseless_circuit`.
- X1 is partial. The state vector (`statevec.rs`, at most 24 qubits) runs every instruction,
  amplitude damping included (`I_ERROR[AMPLITUDE_DAMPING(gamma=…)]`), as quantum trajectories.
  `exact_distribution` follows every branch.
- U9: PECOS 0.2.0 declares `DensityMatrix` and `ProcessMatrix` simulator classes but ships no
  implementation of either.
- X2 and X3 are gaps.

What is left:
- a density-matrix simulator with damping channels (X1);
- PECOS-interface density- and process-matrix simulators (U9);
- QuantumClifford.jl's perturbative expansion `petrajectories` (X2);
- its graph states, entanglement entropy and Clifford enumeration (X3).

**References.**
- Quantumsim 0.2.0 (PyPI; NumPy 2 with `pytools`). It is the reference for the density matrix
  and its amplitude-and-phase damping. Its license is unstated, so it is used only as a test
  reference and none of its code is ported.
- QuantumClifford.jl (master, 0.11.8; MIT). Its algorithms are ported (enumeration, graph
  states, clip/rref entropies, the perturbative branching). Julia does not run here, so its
  tests' pinned values and its stated identities are the check.

**Architecture.** The Rust engine does the work; Python adds thin wrappers in the package's
idiom.
- The density matrix reuses the state vector's compiled program (`statevec::Program`, `Step`),
  applying each step as a superoperator.
- The perturbative expansion reuses the detector-error-model builder's per-channel fault
  effects.
- X3 lives on `Tableau`/`PauliString`, with a Python module `stabilizer_qec.clifford` re-exported
  at the top level.

## Tasks

1. **Density matrix and damping** (X1).
   - New Rust `src/density.rs`, at most 12 qubits: ρ under every `Step` (unitaries, measurements
     and resets as their channels, Pauli noise channels, amplitude damping).
   - New tags `I_ERROR[PHASE_DAMPING(lambda=…)]` and
     `I_ERROR[THERMAL_RELAXATION(t=…, t1=…, t2=…)]`, the latter as Quantumsim's amplitude and
     phase damping (γ = 1 − e^{−t/T1}, λ from 1/T_φ = 1/T2 − 1/(2T1)). They run in the state
     vector's trajectories and in `exact_distribution` too.
   - Python: `Circuit.density_matrix()` (the final state, measurements averaged) and
     `DensityMatrixSimulator` (do gates, peek ρ, probabilities, expectation of a Pauli string,
     purity, partial trace, fidelity).
   - Checks:
     - against Quantumsim's `SparseDM` on random circuits of its gates and damping, ρ equal to
       1e-10;
     - against the state vector averaged over many trajectories, within sampling error;
     - against `exact_distribution`'s outcome probabilities, exactly.
2. **PECOS's density and process matrices** (U9).
   - `stabilizer_qec.pecos.simulators.DensityMatrix` and `ProcessMatrix` on the PECOS
     `Simulator` interface (`run_gate`, `run_circuit`, the gate dictionary PECOS's simulators
     share), running PECOS's QuantumCircuits.
   - The process matrix: a circuit's Pauli transfer matrix, Choi matrix and χ matrix
     (`Circuit.process_matrix(kind=…)`), and the process fidelity.
   - Checks:
     - each PECOS code's logical circuits on the density matrix equal SparseSim's outcomes where
       they are deterministic;
     - the process matrices of known channels (depolarizing, damping, Clifford gates) equal
       their closed forms;
     - Choi, χ and PTM agree with one another.
3. **Perturbative expansion** (X2).
   - New Rust `src/perturb.rs`: every noise channel a site with its no-fault branch and its
     fault branches, each with the detection events and observable flips it causes. Fault
     orders are summed to `order`, by dynamic programming over outcomes.
   - `Circuit.perturbative_distribution(order=…)` gives QuantumClifford's numbers (branch
     weights exact, branches above the order dropped).
   - `symbolic=True` gives each outcome's probability as a polynomial in a noise scale x (every
     channel's probability times x), exact to x^order.
   - `perturbative_logical_error_rate(circuit, order, decoder)` gives the series for a
     decoder's failure.
   - Checks:
     - at full order, equal to `exact_distribution`;
     - at small p, the series against sampling and the exact rate, its error O(p^{order+1});
     - QuantumClifford's `petrajectories` test circuits (Bell-pair purification) with its
       pinned values.
4. **Graph states, entanglement, enumeration** (X3).
   - QuantumClifford's algorithms on this package's tableaux:
     - `graph_state` (stabilizer state to graph plus local Clifford operations: its h, ip and z
       index lists, and the gate sequence back);
     - `Tableau.from_graph`;
     - `entanglement_entropy` (clip, rref and graph algorithms) and `mutual_information`;
     - `bigram`, `canonicalize_clip`;
     - `enumerate_cliffords(n, i)` (QuantumClifford's symplectic Gram–Schmidt indexing),
       `clifford_cardinality`, `enumerate_single_qubit_gates`, `enumerate_phases`.
   - Checks:
     - QuantumClifford's own tests ported;
     - the three entropy algorithms equal each other and the state vector's Schmidt entropy;
     - graph states rebuild the state up to phase;
     - enumeration is a bijection onto Stim's `Tableau.iter_all` for n ≤ 2, with cardinalities
       |Sp(2n, 2)|·4^n.
5. **Bookkeeping.** CHANGELOG, inventory (X1–X3 and U9 done), THIRD_PARTY_NOTICES
   (QuantumClifford.jl), README's feature list.

Each task: tests first where the reference makes that possible. One commit each, and push.
