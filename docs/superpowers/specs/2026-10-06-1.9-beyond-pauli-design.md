# 1.9 "Beyond Pauli noise": coherent errors at circuit level and at scale

Part of the approved 1.6 → 1.9 roadmap (2026-10-06). Revised 2026-10-08 after Jasper's
instruction that new work be something no other tool already does, not a recreation. The
first draft (state vector, coherent errors at d = 3, leakage in the frame sampler, magic-state
injection) was mostly available elsewhere; this revision keeps the parts needed as oracles and
puts a new capability at the centre.

## What already exists (checked 2026-10-08)

- **State-vector and density-matrix simulators** of small codes: Qiskit Aer, Qulacs, quantumsim
  and many others. Not new; we need one only as an oracle.
- **Leakage in Stim**: Riverlane's deltakit-stim (Apache-2.0, a Stim fork) adds `LEAKAGE(p)`,
  `HERALD_LEAKAGE_EVENT` and `RL`, with leakage-aware decoding via its Local Clustering Decoder.
  A leakage-aware frame sampler is therefore not new.
- **Coherent errors, code capacity, large distance**: Bravyi et al. 2018 (Majorana / fermionic
  linear optics; single-axis Z rotations on data qubits, perfect syndrome measurement, d ≤ 37).
  No released code that we found.
- **Coherent errors, circuit level**:
  - pyGSTi (Sandia, arXiv 2504.15128) propagates small Lindbladian error generators through
    Clifford circuits with a BCH/Taylor expansion and returns approximate outcome probabilities
    and syndrome-bit rates up to d = 11. Its authors name logical error rates and decoding as
    future work; it does not produce them.
  - LeBlond et al. (PRR 7, 043184, 2025) estimate logical channels of an idling surface code
    under coherent dephasing to d = 11 by quasi-probability sampling of Clifford circuits. It is
    research code for one model; we found no release.
  - arXiv 2510.23797 estimates detector error models from coherent-noise syndrome data, using
    Majorana and Monte Carlo simulators.

**Gap:** no tool takes an arbitrary Stim circuit with coherent errors, samples its detectors and
observables at circuit level and at the distances people care about, and decodes those samples.
Nor does any tool tell you how wrong the Pauli twirl (what Stim and every decoder assume) is for
your circuit. 1.9 builds that.

## Headline: the coherent sampler

### Notation

- Coherent errors are written in the circuit as tagged identity errors, which stock Stim reads as
  identities, so circuits stay Stim-readable:
  - `I_ERROR[R_X(theta=θ)] q`, `I_ERROR[R_Y(theta=θ)] q`, `I_ERROR[R_Z(theta=θ)] q`;
  - `II_ERROR[R_ZZ(theta=θ)] a b`, `II_ERROR[R_XX(theta=θ)] a b`, `II_ERROR[R_YY(theta=θ)] a b`;
  - more generally `I_ERROR[R_PAULI(theta=θ, pauli=XZ…)]` targets.
- Each tag means exp(−iθP).
- The Clifford engines (Stim, our frame sampler, error models) ignore these tags; the coherent
  sampler and the state vector honour them.
- An unknown tag stays inert. A malformed known tag is an error naming the line.

### Method

The method is a twirl-importance sampler with exact spacetime interference. Write each coherent
location k as exp(−iθ_k P_k) = c_k I − i s_k P_k. Expand the circuit as a sum over fault sets F
with amplitudes a_F = Π_{k∈F}(−i s_k) Π_{k∉F} c_k. Then:

1. **The importance distribution is the Pauli twirl.** The twirl q(F, m) = |a_F|² p₀(m ⊕ x_F) is
   exactly what the frame sampler draws when each coherent location fires P_k with probability
   sin²θ_k. Here p₀ is the noiseless record distribution and x_F is the record flips of F.
   Stochastic Pauli noise rides along unchanged.
2. **Interference.** The true probability of a record m is
   Σ_{F,F'} a_F a_{F'}* ⟨φ|O_{F'}† Π_m O_F|φ⟩.
   - A cross term is nonzero only when F' = F Δ G, where G is a set of locations whose
     propagated Pauli flips no measurement. This counts hidden ones: every reset is a hidden
     measurement, and the circuit end is a hidden Z measurement of every live qubit.
   - These G form a GF(2) linear space, the circuit's *spacetime kernel*.
3. **The cross term's value is exact and cheap.** For each kernel element, R_G is a product of
   Z's on measured (or hidden) outcomes, with a fixed phase. The ratio
   T(F, FΔG, m) / q(F, m) is the product of three factors:
   - Π_{g∈G∖F} (i·tan θ_g) · Π_{g∈G∩F} (−i / tan θ_g);
   - (−1)^(Σ_g a_g + fixed_G), where a_g says whether P_g anticommutes with the shot's *fault*
     frame (faults only, no randomisation) just before location g;
   - (−1)^(m′·z_G) · phase_G, where m′ is the shot's noiseless-branch outcomes (the record with
     fault flips removed).

   The sampler runs two frames, one for faults and one for the reference randomisation, which
   gives a_g and m′ per shot with bit-parallel words.
4. **Bounded weights.** Each unordered pair {F, FΔG} is counted once, from the side with the
   larger twirl probability (factor 2, or 1 each on a tie). So every term is at most 1 in
   magnitude; counting both sides would let 1/tan θ terms blow the variance up by the number of
   locations.
5. **Exact merging.** Coherent locations whose propagated Paulis are equal up to sign with no
   location between them (same flip vector, empty z_G) compose exactly:
   exp(−iθ₁P) exp(∓iθ₂P) = exp(−i(θ₁ ± θ₂)P). They are merged before sampling, unless a
   stochastic error that anticommutes with P can occur between them; in that case they are kept
   separate and handled by the kernel terms. This removes the commonest weight-2 kernel elements,
   for example Z before and after a CZ.
6. **Truncation.**
   - The per-shot weight sums kernel elements built from up to `order` local generators that
     touch the shot's fault set.
   - Elements far from every fault give a nearly constant factor. Self-normalising,
     p̂ = Σ f·w / Σ w, cancels it.
   - Local generators are found by meet-in-the-middle over the sparse flip vectors: singles with
     an empty flip vector, then pairs with equal flip vectors, then pairs of pairs with equal XOR,
     restricted to locations that share a flipped bit.
   - `order = None` with a small circuit enumerates the whole kernel, which makes the estimator
     exact (unbiased).
7. **Output** is the same as the detector sampler's (detection events, observable flips), plus
   one real weight per shot.
   - `sinter`-style counting becomes weighted counting with a standard error from the weight
     variance.
   - Every decoder in the package decodes the samples, and failures are counted with their
     weights.

### Coherence-aware error model

`Circuit.detector_error_model(coherent="merge")` is the twirl after exact merging (step 5): a
merged class contributes sin²(Σ±θ) rather than Σ sin²θ. Decoding coherent samples with this model
against the plain twirl model is the "coherence-aware decoding" result.

### Exact state vector (the oracle)

- `src/statevec.rs` covers every Stim instruction, `REPEAT` and feedback.
- It honours the coherent tags plus `I[T]`, `I[T_DAG]`, `I[R_X/R_Y/R_Z(theta=…)]` and
  `I[U3(theta=…, phi=…, lambda=…)]`, with trajectories for stochastic channels.
- It handles up to 24 qubits in use (indices never touched are compacted; Stim's d = 3 surface
  code is 17), with multi-threaded amplitude loops.
- `Circuit.compile_exact_sampler(seed)` has the same `sample` signature as the detector sampler.
- Verified on Clifford circuits (detector statistics equal the frame sampler's within shot
  noise), single gates (their matrices), and ≤ 8-qubit circuits (a dense-matrix oracle).

## Verification of the coherent sampler

1. **Exact on tiny circuits.** Enumerate every F and every noiseless branch m′ with the full
   kernel. The weighted distribution over (detectors, observables) equals the state vector's to
   1e-10, on random small circuits with coherent tags, stochastic noise, resets and mid-circuit
   measurements (Hypothesis).
2. **Statistical agreement.** Agreement within 4σ against the state vector on:
   - the repetition-code memory up to d = 11 (21 qubits) at circuit level;
   - the d = 3 rotated surface code (17 qubits), with Z over-rotation on data and ZZ crosstalk on
     CZ pairs;
   - the Steane code memory.
3. **Twirl limit.** With θ = 0 at all locations, the weights are all 1 and the samples equal
   the frame sampler's, bit for bit, at the same seed.
4. **Code capacity versus Bravyi et al.** With single-axis Z rotation on data, perfect syndrome
   measurement and d up to 25, reproduce the qualitative findings: the logical-level error
   approaches the twirl as d grows, and the threshold is near the twirled one. Compare against
   points read from their figures and marked as such.
5. **Convergence in `order`.** For each published figure, the reported logical error rate
   changes by less than its error bar from `order` k to k + 1.

## Leakage (secondary, kept small)

- Leakage is written Stim-readably as `I_ERROR[LEAK(p=…)] q`, `I_ERROR[SEEP(p=…)] q` and
  `II_ERROR[LEAK_TRANSPORT(p=…)] a b`.
- It is carried in the frame sampler as a leaked flag per qubit with Google's partner-depolarising
  model. A leaked qubit measures 1 (option: random), and reset clears it.
- Measurements of leaked qubits are heralded as erasures to every decoder.
- This parallels deltakit-stim, and the README says so. The differences:
  - circuits stay readable by stock Stim;
  - the model is cross-checked against a qutrit state vector on small circuits;
  - leakage-aware decoding works in every decoder we ship.

## Out of scope

- Magic-state injection studies; the state vector runs `I[T]`, and tutorial 05 shows one small
  example.
- Amplitude damping inside the coherent sampler. It is not unitary; the state vector covers it.
- Feedback inside the coherent sampler. It is an error for now; the state vector covers it.
- Stabiliser-rank methods; GPU.
