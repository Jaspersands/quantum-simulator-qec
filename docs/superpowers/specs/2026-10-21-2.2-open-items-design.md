# 2.2: the items 2.1.0 left open — design

**Why.** 2.1.0's CHANGELOG ("Not ported") and the parity inventory
(`docs/superpowers/notes/2026-10-09-parity-inventory.md`) list what is still missing from
other tools, or not equal to them. The standing rules (parity with every tool, plus one
capability nobody else has, per release) make each of these work for 2.2. Research for each item
was done on 2026-10-21 against the references themselves; the scripts and fixtures are in the
session scratchpad under `plan22/`.

## Headline (new): exact coherent class sums at any kernel size

The coherent sampler (`src/coherent/`) sums a fault class exactly only when the kernel has at
most twelve generators (`SMALL_KERNEL`); past that it truncates. On a d = 3 surface memory over
three rounds (fourteen generators) the silent logical rate reads 0.00161 ± 0.00013 against the
exact 0.00258.

The class sum is a sum over c ∈ GF(2)^k of a product of factors, with three kinds of factor:
- one per location, f_l(⊕_{i∈S_l} c_i), where f_l(1) is the location's toggle ratio;
- a phase i^{q(c)}, with q(c) = Σ a_i c_i + 2 Σ b_ij c_i c_j mod 4;
- a shot sign Π s_i^{c_i}.

The norm is the same graph with |f|² and no phase. This was checked on 3,000 random subsets of
each of 13 circuits, mixed rotations and ZZ crosstalk among them.

Such a sum is exactly contracted by bucket (variable) elimination along a min-fill order, in
O(k·2^w) per shot, where w is the elimination width. The widths:
- surface memories: 3 at d3 r3, 6 at d5 r5, 9 at d7 r7;
- code-capacity d ≤ 15: at most 10.

The plan is built once at compile time. Each shot fills the factor tables and eliminates. When
the plan's table size is over a budget (`max_contraction`, 2^16 entries by default), the
sampler keeps 2.1's truncation and says so (`exact_weights` false).

The joint sampler's cross terms are overlaps of exact-engine states, not products of factors, so
it keeps its threshold. That is documented, not changed.

**Done when:**
- every shot's weight equals the whole-kernel weight (`Program::weight(shot, full_kernel)`) to
  1e-10 for k from 13 to 20, mixed X/Y/Z, S, H and CZ (b_ij ≠ 0), groups, and merge on;
- random small circuits with k > 12 equal the state vector exactly;
- the d3 r3 surface silent rate lands within 5σ of 0.00258.

## Parity

### 1. tqec: its own compile pipeline, byte for byte

Port tqec 0.2.0's compile path and tqecd 0.2.1 (both Apache-2.0) in Python as
`stabilizer_qec.tqec._compile`. About 7,100 of tqec's lines run on that path. It runs on our
`BlockGraph` and our Stim-compatible `Circuit`. tqec uses only numpy, stim (Circuit,
CircuitInstruction, CircuitRepeatBlock, PauliString, Tableau, gate_data) and tqecd on that path,
and this package already has all of those.

`compile_block_graph(...).generate_stim_circuit(k, noise_model)` then gives tqec's text, byte for
byte, for every gallery graph in both conventions:
- Hadamard pipes (temporal and spatial) and spatial junction cubes now compile (CZ, stability,
  move/rotation, three CNOTs, Steane encoding);
- CNOT at k = 1 has tqec's 81 qubits, not the engine's 113.

**Y cubes.** tqec 0.2.0 raises `NotImplementedError` for them, so we raise the same.

**The engine.** It stays behind `BlockGraph.compile(distance)`.

**Hash order.** tqec emits detectors in `list(set(...))` order, and its hashes are of integers
only. The port keeps the same hash chain (GridQubit `(x, y)`, frozen-dataclass Measurement,
Detector's frozenset).

**Python 3.9.** tqec needs 3.10+ (`match`, `itertools.pairwise`); the port rewrites those.

**tqec's own bugs.** tqec gives nondeterministic observables for `cz("XI -> XZ")` and
nondeterministic detectors for `cz("ZZ -> ZZ")` at fixed_boundary k = 2. If the cause is local, the
port fixes it and the inventory states the deviation; otherwise it reproduces tqec.

**Done when** the recorded fixtures match byte for byte. The fixtures are tqec's circuits with
`do_not_use_database=True`:
- 14 graphs × 2 conventions at k = 1;
- selected k = 2 cases;
- a temporal Hadamard;
- rotated graphs;
- two CNOTs in a row;
- `observables=None` and explicit observables;
- `block_temporal_height` variants.

### 2. PyMatching's engine exactly, and what rides on it

`src/pm_exact/` is a line-for-line Rust transliteration of PyMatching 2.4.0's sparse blossom.
It covers:
- the flooder;
- the radix-heap tracker with its last-in-first-out ties;
- the matcher, including blossom cycle direction;
- the two-ended search flooder;
- `decode_detection_events_to_edges`, with negative edges first and swap-removal;
- extraction by detection event.

It has 2^24 weight levels and two graph orders: 2.4.0's (the model's neighbour order, boundary
first) and 2.2.2's (neighbours by node, the PyMatching that Chromobius 1.1.1 builds).

It powers:
- the PyMatching-API `Matching` (`stabilizer_qec.pymatching` / `_matching.py` / `src/user_graph.rs`),
  so `decode`, `decode_batch`, `decode_to_edges_array` and `decode_to_matched_dets_array` equal
  PyMatching's on every shot, ties included;
- the Chromobius port (2.2.2 order). Handed 2.2.2-ordered edges, Chromobius's predictions came back
  on all 65,000 shots checked;
- `pymatching animate`.

The package's own decoders (`src/sparse`: correlated matching, windows, `sq` DEM decoders) keep
their engine, fingerprints and benchmarks.

**`pymatching animate`** takes PyMatching's flags and messages, all five held/growth/pixel flags
required, with 1000 weight levels. It writes `frame_%05d.svg` in PyMatching's drawing:
- grey edges, boundary edges dashed;
- region polygons coloured by state;
- orange collisions, tree, blossom and match lines;
- float32 `%g` numbers.

PyMatching draws regions in heap-address order. So frames are compared as sorted lines, and the
frame count and names must be equal.

**Done when** ordered edge lists and pairs equal PyMatching 2.4's on surface codes, colour
matching graphs, boundaries, negative weights and user-built graphs. Chromobius must have zero
differences on macOS across the sweep. Linux keeps its documented tolerance, because Chromobius's
own hash-map order differs there.

### 3. Command lines Chromobius and Tesseract have

**Chromobius** (`src/main.cc`):
- `predict`: `--dem`, `--in`, `--in_format` (default b8), `--in_includes_appended_observables`,
  `--out`, `--out_format` (default 01);
- `benchmark`: the same plus `--obs_in`/`--obs_in_format`;
- `describe_decoder`: `--in` or `--circuit`, `--out`.

**Tesseract** (`tesseract_main.cc` flags):
- input, detector orders, sampling, files, search, multipass and sparsify flags;
- its `num_shots`/`num_low_confidence`/`num_errors`/`total_time_seconds` output;
- `--stats-out` JSON.

Neither wheel ships these commands. Each is checked against the reference's Python API on the
same inputs, with text formats taken from the C++ source.

**Tesseract's A* takes any number of observables.** Today the flat model's `u64` caps it at 64.
It is built from the unrolled program with `ObsBits`, and the observables come back packed.

### 4. Tsim's ZX graphs and `to_matrix` with measurements

Port Tsim's `core/parse.py` and `core/instructions.py` builders (Apache-2.0) against a small
graph interface:
- add_vertex, add_edge, type, phase, row, qubit, neighbours, scalar, phase variables.

The interface is backed by our own ZX graph, or by `pyzx_param.GraphS` when that is installed
(Tsim's type). It provides:
- `get_graph()`, `get_sampling_graph(sample_detectors=)`;
- `diagram(type="pyzx"|"pyzx-dets"|"pyzx-meas")`. Drawing goes through pyzx; the reduced forms
  need `pyzx_param` and raise `ImportError` without it;
- `tcount`, `to_tensor`;
- `to_matrix` with measurements, contracted in numpy, each parameter read as 0, as Tsim does.

**Done when** vertex ids, types, phases, phase variables, positions, edges and scalar equal
Tsim's on a corpus, and `to_matrix` agrees with Tsim's under `allclose`.

**Declined, with the reason recorded:** Tsim's bit-for-bit JAX shots. They need its PCG64 noise
draws, channel simplification, `pyzx_param`'s simplifier order, the stabilizer-rank terms, the
threefry calls, and XLA's complex64 sum order. Tsim itself is reproducible only at a fixed batch
size.

### 5. CUDA-Q QEC

**`dem_chunks` and the extended DEM** (cudaq-qec 0.8.1). This is `py_extended_dem.cpp`'s Python
surface:
- `SeamId` (FNV-1a 32 plus a name registry), `ExtendedDem(Seam)`, the spec classes;
- `dem_stitch*`, `dem_close*`, `dem_merge_duplicate_columns`, `dem_chunk_rounds`;
- `dem_chunks_to_{rounds,detector_round,d_sparse,o_sparse,pcm}`;
- `extended_dem_from_css_matrices`, with `css_code_matrices` and `dem_from_css_matrices`.

`decoder_config.dem_chunks` gets the YAML and checks from `config.cpp:366-606`.
`expand_dem_chunks` returns the closed model, and `configure_decoders` expands it. It is numpy
only; `dem_chunks_to_pcm` needs scipy, as CUDA-Q's does.

**Done when** CUDA-Q's 12 chunk YAML tests, about 24 extended-DEM tests and its C++
closed-form/sizes cases are replayed. "Close" is also cross-checked against our circuit→DEM.

**`trt_decoder` on the CPU.** It reads ONNX with a zero-dependency protobuf reader and evaluates
in numpy. The ops: Gemm, MatMul, Add, Relu, Sigmoid, Identity, Cast, Reshape, Flatten,
QuantizeLinear/DequantizeLinear (int8 and float8e4m3fn).

It has CUDA-Q's parameter schema and its errors:
- exactly one load path;
- one input, one output, each float32 or uint8;
- a dynamic batch dimension needs `batch_size`.

Its decoding behaviour is CUDA-Q's:
- `decode` zero-pads to the model batch;
- `decode_batch` runs in chunks;
- uint8 input is thresholded and uint8 output becomes 0/1;
- the `O` and `global_decoder` residual path.

`engine_load_path` and `engine_save_path` are refused by name, since a TensorRT engine is a
GPU-specific binary. Precision options log CUDA-Q's warnings.

**Done when** the evaluator agrees with onnxruntime (installed only in a scratch venv) on CUDA-Q's
fixtures, which are rebuilt byte-identical to their LFS hashes. The surface-code model, fetched
read-only from the cudaqx repository, must reproduce CUDA-Q's 30 recorded PyTorch outputs to 1e-4.

### 10. ldpc's BP+LSD: ldpc is nondeterministic, so test by reachability

ldpc's `merge_list` is a `tsl::robin_set<LsdCluster*>` iterated in heap-address order. One
syndrome decoded 40 times in one process gave two answers, interleaved; ours gave one answer
120 times. Across 6,000 window decodes, ours was always among ldpc's observed answers.

The fix has three parts:
- A chooser hook in `merge_intersecting` (`src/lsd.rs`). The default keeps today's order.
- A debug method `_merge_order_outcomes(syndrome, max_leaves, target=None)`. It runs BP once, then walks the merge orders fewest departures from `decode`'s order first (every order that departs from it at one merge, then at two, and so on), for at most `max_leaves` orders or until one reaches `target`, and returns the corrections reached. A syndrome no correction explains raises `decode`'s ValueError.
- New tests. ldpc's answers over 50 decodes, on the 15 captured windows, must all lie in that set,
  as must ours. `tools/decoder_check.py` and `tests/test_quits_differential.py` stop waiving
  `last_tied` shots (44–82% of decodes) and the `<= 1`/`<= 2` tolerances, and check reachability
  instead.

On Linux, libstdc++ hashes a pointer to itself, so ldpc may be deterministic and equal to ours.
CI records which.

### 11. Undetectable-error search memory

The 2.1.0 note (456 MB, 8.7 s against Stim's 139 MB, 1.6 s) was measured before the rewrite and
is corrected. Today ours is faster than Stim and uses half its memory growth. QUITS's cardinal
circuit also depends on `PYTHONHASHSEED`.

Remaining work:
- Store states as columns: a pool of detectors with offsets; parallel obs, parent and edge
  vectors; an open-addressing id table; no queue. This took a prototype from 248 to 110 MB and
  from 0.74 to 0.41 s.
- Replace the 20M-state cap. Stim has none, and the cap makes [[90,8,10]] at (6, 6) give up
  where Stim keeps searching. The replacement is a memory budget, `max_memory_bytes`, defaulting
  to half the machine's RAM.
