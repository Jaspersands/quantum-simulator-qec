# 1.9 "Beyond Pauli noise": an exact small simulator, coherent errors, and leakage

Part of the approved 1.6 → 1.9 roadmap (2026-10-06).

## Why

Every simulator in the package, like Stim, assumes Pauli noise on Clifford circuits. Real
hardware has coherent over-rotations, amplitude damping and leakage, and fault-tolerant
algorithms need non-Clifford gates. How wrong is the Pauli picture for a small code? Answering
it needs an exact simulator, and the answer is a result worth publishing on the site.

## Decisions

- **Circuits stay Stim-readable.** Non-Clifford and non-Pauli operations are written as tagged
  Stim instructions that Stim treats as identities: `I[T] q`, `I[T_DAG] q`, `I[R_Z(theta=0.1)] q`
  (likewise `R_X`, `R_Y`, `U3(theta=…, phi=…, lambda=…)`), `I_ERROR[COHERENT_RZ(theta=…)] q`,
  `I_ERROR[AMPLITUDE_DAMPING(gamma=…)] q`, `I_ERROR[LEAK(p=…)] q`, `I_ERROR[SEEP(p=…)] q`,
  `II_ERROR[LEAK_TRANSPORT(p=…)] q1 q2`. Stim (and this package's Clifford engines) run them as
  the Clifford/Pauli circuit around them; the new simulators honour them. Parsing them is
  checked: an unknown tag on `I`/`I_ERROR` stays inert, a malformed known tag is an error.
- **Exact means state vector**, up to 24 qubits in use (256 MB of amplitudes; qubit indices a
  circuit never touches are compacted away, so Stim's d = 3 surface code, indices to 25, is 17), with mid-circuit
  measurement, reset, feedback, detectors and observables; trajectories (quantum jumps) for
  non-unitary channels, so memory stays one state.

## Scope

1. **`src/statevec.rs`**: a state-vector simulator over the whole circuit language (every Stim
   gate, measurement, reset, feedback, `REPEAT`), plus the tagged operations; multi-threaded
   amplitude loops; `Circuit.compile_exact_sampler(seed)` with the same `sample` signature as
   the detector sampler. Verified: on Clifford circuits its detector statistics equal the frame
   sampler's (and Stim's) within shot noise; single-gate unitaries equal their matrices; T-gate
   circuits against hand-computed expectations and against a dense-matrix oracle on ≤ 8 qubits.
2. **Coherent errors versus their Pauli twirl.** For the repetition code, the d = 3 rotated
   surface code (17 qubits) and the Steane-code memory: logical error rate under coherent
   over-rotation `R_Z(θ)` after every gate against the Pauli-twirled channel (`Z_ERROR(sin²θ)`),
   swept over θ and rounds. A figure for the site and the report, and tutorial 05.
3. **Leakage in the frame sampler.** A per-qubit leaked flag carried in the bit-parallel sampler:
   `LEAK` sets it, `SEEP` clears it, a two-qubit gate with a leaked partner applies a random
   Pauli to the other qubit (Google's model), measurement of a leaked qubit reads 1 (option:
   random), reset clears it, `LEAK_TRANSPORT` moves it. Leakage-aware decoding: measurements of
   leaked qubits are heralded as erasures and reweight the matcher (the existing heralded-erasure
   path). Verified against the state-vector simulator with a three-level qutrit model on small
   circuits, and against Google's leakage results qualitatively (leakage-removal resets restore
   Λ).
4. **Magic-state injection.** A d = 3 colour-code injection with `I[T]` and post-selection,
   simulated exactly; logical infidelity against the injected error rate, as an example of the
   non-Clifford path.

## Out of scope

Stabilizer-rank or extended-stabilizer methods for many T gates; density-matrix simulation
(trajectories cover the noise); GPU.

## Verification

Oracles: dense-matrix simulation on tiny circuits; Clifford-limit agreement with Stim; the
analytic coherent-error results for the repetition code (known closed forms at one round);
leakage model cross-checked between the frame sampler and the qutrit state vector.
