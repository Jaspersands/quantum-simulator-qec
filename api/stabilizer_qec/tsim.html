stabilizer_qec.tsim

Tsim's interface on this package's circuits and samplers, with Tsim's conventions.

  • Circuit: Tsim's circuit, a subclass of stabilizer_qec.Circuit (isinstance holds for both), read from Tsim's program text (T 0, T_DAG 1, TPP X0*Z1, R_Z(0.25) 0 in half-turns, R_ZZ(0.5) 0 1, R_PAULI(0.3) X0*Y1, U3(0.3, 0.2, 0.1) 0, CCZ 0 1 2, Tsim's tagged S[T] 0 and I[R_X(theta=0.25*pi)] 0, and this package's I[T] 0 and radians), from_stim_program and from_file (shorthand too); append takes Tsim's gates with their arguments in half-turns and append_from_stim_program_text its text; str() and repr() are Tsim's shorthand; + and += take a stim.Circuit or stabilizer_qec.Circuit (Tsim's tagged Stim read as its gates); what Tsim's methods return is a tsim.Circuit (copy, slices, *, flattened, without_noise, inverse, ...), and stim_circuit a plain stabilizer_qec.Circuit. tcount, is_clifford and inverse are Tsim's; to_matrix is Tsim's map on the qubits the circuit names, a state column when every qubit is reset first, and, of a circuit with measurements, detectors or observables (one that XORs no measurement is a factor of 2), resets after a qubit's first operation, feedback, noise on a qubit before its reset or a product rotation naming a qubit twice, Tsim's ZX tensor with every measurement outcome and error bit read as 0 (a circuit with Pauli products or sweep bits has its ZX graph built first, so its errors are Tsim's); diagram defaults to the timeline SVG, its tagged gates labelled, with Tsim's height, width and zoomable for the notebook view, and draws the ZX graph for "pyzx", "pyzx-dets" and "pyzx-meas".
  • Circuit.get_graph and Circuit.get_sampling_graph (stabilizer_qec.tsim.zx): Tsim's ZX graphs, equal to Tsim's vertex for vertex (numbers, types, phases as Fractions, phase variables, positions, edges, scalar). They are pyzx_param's GraphS when pyzx_param imports, else this package's ZXGraph (backend="own" or "pyzx_param" chooses; ZXGraph.to_pyzx() gives a pyzx graph). zx.tensorfy contracts a graph in numpy. Choosing the default backend (and zx.pyzx_param_available()) imports pyzx_param when it is installed, and importing pyzx_param calls numpy.set_printoptions(suppress=True) for the whole process, as importing Tsim does; backend="own" imports nothing. A record entry from before the circuit's first measurement (M 0 then DETECTOR rec[-2]) raises zx.parse.MeasurementRecordError, an IndexError as Tsim's is and a ValueError that names the instruction.
  • CompiledDetectorSampler (Circuit.compile_detector_sampler): Tsim's convention, each detector's and observable's value, the parity of its measurements (X 0, M 0, DETECTOR rec[-1] reads True), not Stim's flip from a reference run; use_detector_reference_sample / use_observable_reference_sample give the flips (one noiseless shot each call is the reference, as Tsim's; on a Clifford circuit Stim's events exactly). CompiledMeasurementSampler (Circuit.compile_sampler): the records. batch_size and postselection_mask are checked as Tsim checks them and strategy is accepted (Tsim does not check it either); none changes a sample (they are performance knobs of Tsim's JAX programs; every row is a full sample). The shots are this package's samplers' (the frame sampler on a Clifford circuit, the stabilizer-rank sampler on a non-Clifford one), so they match Tsim's in distribution, not bit for bit. stabilizer_qec.Circuit's own samplers keep Stim's conventions.
  • sampler.CompiledStateProbs: P(state | f) for each of a batch of noise draws f, of a measurement record or (sample_detectors) of the detectors' and observables' values.
  • Circuit.detector_error_model and get_detector_error_model: Tsim's model, which allows non-deterministic observables (error(0.5) D0 L0); with it decompose_errors raises.
  • Tsim's encoders, TransversalEncoder and its [[7, 1, 3]] Steane (SteaneEncoder) and [[17, 1, 5]] colour-code (ColorEncoder5) instances, which encode a state-preparation program into code blocks and a program into its transversal form (detectors and observables onto the code's stabilizers and logicals); their circuit is a tsim.Circuit.
  • shorthand_to_stim and stim_to_shorthand: Tsim's shorthand to and from its tagged Stim (stim_to_shorthand reads this package's tagged forms too), and tsim_to_native: that Stim as this package's operations (Tsim's S[T] is I[T] here, its half-turn rotations radians of exp(−iθP)).

Where it differs from Tsim:

  • Shots and noise draws are this package's, equal to Tsim's in distribution (Tsim's are JAX's); deterministic values, models, matrices and noiseless probabilities are Tsim's.
  • An angle appended as an int prints as a float (R_Z(1.0), Tsim R_Z(1)); a tag on an appended T, T_DAG, TPP or TPP_DAG is not kept, nor the spacing inside a tagged rotation's (SPP[R_PAULI( theta=0.3*pi )] is quoted SPP[R_PAULI(theta=0.3*pi)]); the angle literals of a stim.Circuit are read as floats, as Tsim reads its program text (Tsim's from_stim_program keeps the stim circuit's 0.30000000000000001*pi, the phase 30000000000000001/10^17, where this is 3/10). Text that does not parse raises this package's ValueError naming the line (SPP: bad Pauli product '2' for R_ZZ(0.25) 0 1 2 3, Stim's "Gate SPP only takes Pauli targets").
  • A product rotation is held one product a line, I[R_PAULI(theta=t, pauli=XY)] 0 1 in radians, and the rest of Tsim's instruction is kept in how that is written: a factor's letter is lower case where it has a ! (SPP[T] !X0*Y1 is pauli=xY), the SPP_DAG of a rotation has its arguments in the other order (SPP_DAG[R_PAULI(theta=0.3*pi)] X0 is I[R_PAULI(pauli=X, theta=-0.47123889803846897)] 0), and R_PAULI(±0.25) is ±π/8 with further digits (0.392699081698724139; TPP's is 0.39269908169872414). That is the text circuit[i], stim_circuit, to_file and this package's own errors show. Read back, the instruction is Tsim's: it prints as Tsim's (SPP_DAG[R_PAULI(theta=0.3*pi)] X0, TPP !X0), and it fuses with its neighbours, counts towards R_PAULI's 64 qubits and is quoted in errors as in Tsim. Text in this form is read as the core reads it: _ and I are identity letters (I[R_PAULI(theta=0.1, pauli=X_Z)] 0 1 2 is Tsim's R_PAULI(...) X0*Z2), spaces and empty arguments in a tag are skipped (but a pair rotation's empty first argument, its SPP_DAG, below), I[T()] is I[T], R_PAULI on II is the product rotation it is on I (II[R_PAULI(theta=0.1, pauli=XZ)] 0 1 is Tsim's R_PAULI(...) X0*Z1, its inverse that of the I form on II), and on I_ERROR or II_ERROR it is a coherent error, no gate. A product rotation whose letters are all identities (pauli=__) is no operation and is left out of the print, as the core, the graph and the model read it (Tsim would read II[R_PAULI(...)] 1 2 as a plain II naming qubits 1 and 2). So the print, read back by Tsim or by tsim.Circuit, has that instruction fewer, and its num_qubits is that of the qubits it names, without one only the product names (H 0 then II[R_PAULI(theta=0.1, pauli=__)] 1 2 has len 2 and num_qubits 3, its print H 0 1 and 1), as it is without one only an identity letter names (I[R_PAULI(theta=0.1, pauli=X_)] 0 1 prints R_PAULI(...) X0, num_qubits 1, not 2): the print and repr of such a circuit do not give it back under ==, though what they give has its graph, tcount, matrix, model and inverse. A pair rotation II[R_ZZ(theta=t)] 0 1 2 3 (R_XX, R_YY) is Tsim's R_ZZ(a) 0 1 and R_ZZ(a) 2 3; its SPP_DAG is written with an empty argument first, II[R_ZZ(, theta=-t)] 3 2 1 0, as a product rotation's has its arguments in the other order, and prints as Tsim's SPP_DAG[R_PAULI(theta=a*pi)] Z3*Z2 Z1*Z0: so the inverse of a pair rotation, or of a product rotation on II, is one instruction, as Tsim's, and its inverse the circuit again.
  • Two product rotations with the same angle in a row stay two lines (Tsim's Stim text fuses them into one, which its shorthand misprints), as do a pair rotation's TPPs on several pairs (TPP_DAG X0*X1 then TPP_DAG X2*X3, the inverse TPP X3*X2 then TPP X1*X0, where Tsim prints TPP X3*X2 X1*X0), and a product naming one qubit twice prints as R_PAULI(0.3) X0*X0 (Tsim's shorthand R_XX(0.3) 0 0 does not read back). They are one instruction to the ZX graph, as in Tsim, so R_PAULI's limit of 64 qubits is counted over them (33 R_ZZ(0.3) lines in a row are Tsim's error, "got 66").
  • tcount is the graph's, as Tsim's: a coherent error (I_ERROR[R_Z(...)]) is not a gate (stabilizer_qec.Circuit.tcount counts its phase).
  • An angle is kept as it was written, as Tsim keeps it (the radians this package stores carry the digits that give the literal back), so R_Z(0.3) is the phase 3/10 of Tsim's graph and R_PAULI(0.25) is Tsim's R_PAULI, not TPP: its graph has the scalar phase -1/8 (+1/8 for -0.25) that TPP's has not, and its errors are R_PAULI's. An angle whose radians are past a float's range (from about 5.7e307 half-turns) is refused; Tsim takes it.
  • diagram("pyzx") needs pyzx or pyzx_param to draw; "pyzx-dets" and "pyzx-meas" need pyzx_param (their graphs are reduced by its full_reduce). to_matrix is at most 2^22 entries and, through the ZX tensor (a circuit with measurements, detectors or observables, ...), its spiders at most 24 legs and the tensors of its contraction at most 2^26 entries (1 GiB of complex128, which numpy's tensordot copies). A chain of more than 24 correlated errors builds, its graph and matrix Tsim's, but its 2^k probabilities (Tsim's channel_probs entry, an array) are kept as the chain (zx.instructions.CorrelatedChainProbs): an entry each, and ValueError read as an array.
  • circuit[i] is this package's instruction (I[T] 0), not a stim.CircuitInstruction in Tsim's tagged form (S[T] 0).
  • to_matrix of a circuit with an instruction Tsim's graph builders cannot read (a tag the core leaves inert, I[foo(x)] 0, which get_graph refuses as Tsim does) is the unitary engine's matrix, where Tsim's to_matrix raises that error (and a refusal of a Pauli product after that instruction is not raised either), unless a product rotation names a qubit twice (TPP X0*Y0*X0, whose sign the engine does not keep): then it raises that error, as Tsim's. Where a product leaves a qubit out of Tsim's graph (an identity letter's, pauli=X_, or one SPP X0*X0 cancels on), that matrix is Tsim's of the circuit without the instruction up to a global phase, as the engine's: its qubits are those Tsim's graph of that circuit has lanes for, as every to_matrix is.
class Circuit(stabilizer_qec._circuit.Circuit):

Tsim's circuit: a stabilizer_qec.Circuit (so isinstance(c, stabilizer_qec.Circuit) and every method of this package's circuits) that reads and writes Tsim's program text.

It is built from Tsim's shorthand or its tagged Stim (T 0, T_DAG 1, TPP X0*Z1, R_Z(0.25) 0 in half-turns, R_ZZ(0.5) 0 1, R_PAULI(0.3) X0*Y1, U3(0.3, 0.2, 0.1) 0, CCZ 0 1 2, S[T] 0, I[R_X(theta=0.25*pi)] 0), from this package's form (I[T] 0, I[R_X(theta=0.39269908169872414)] 0 in radians), or from a stim.Circuit or stabilizer_qec.Circuit in either; it holds this package's form, and str() and repr() give Tsim's shorthand (T 0, R_X(0.1) 0). What Tsim's methods return is a tsim.Circuit here too (copy, slices, +, *, flattened, without_noise, inverse, ...); stim_circuit is a plain stabilizer_qec.Circuit.

Its samplers are Tsim's: compile_detector_sampler reports each detector's and observable's value (the parity of its measurements), not its flip from a reference run as Stim's does (use_detector_reference_sample / use_observable_reference_sample give the flips); detector_error_model allows non-deterministic observables. A stabilizer_qec.Circuit keeps Stim's conventions.

>>> from stabilizer_qec import tsim
>>> c = tsim.Circuit("R 0\nH 0\nT 0\nH 0\nM 0")
>>> print(c)
R 0
H 0
T 0
H 0
M 0

def detector_error_model( self, *, decompose_errors: bool = False, flatten_loops: bool = False, allow_gauge_detectors: bool = False, approximate_disjoint_errors: Any = False, ignore_decomposition_failures: bool = False, block_decomposition_from_introducing_remnant_edges: bool = False, allow_non_deterministic_observables: bool = True) -> stabilizer_qec.DetectorErrorModel:

Return a stim.DetectorErrorModel describing the error processes in the circuit.

Unlike the stim.Circuit.detector_error_model() method, this method allows for non-deterministic observables (an observable a non-Clifford gate leaves random): the observables become detectors, the model is built with allow_gauge_detectors, and they become observables again, gauge errors on observables alone dropped (tsim.get_detector_error_model). decompose_errors=True is not supported with it and raises ValueError, as Tsim's; allow_non_deterministic_observables=False (this package's keyword) gives Stim's model. As Tsim's, the model is Stim's analysis of Tsim's tagged form of the circuit: T is read as S, a rotation or U3 as I, a Pauli-product rotation as SPP (so R 0, H 0, T 0, H 0, M 0 measures a random bit).

The other arguments are Stim's.

def to_matrix(self, *, endian: str = 'big') -> numpy.ndarray:

Convert circuit to matrix representation, as Tsim's to_matrix: the map the circuit's gates apply to the qubits Tsim's ZX graph has a lane for, those its instructions name, noise, identities and coordinates among them (a qubit no instruction names is dropped, so R_ZZ(0.5) 0 2 is 4 × 4, and so is one that only a product that cancels on it names, TPP Z0*Z0 being [[1]] as in Tsim and SPP X0*Z1*Z1 2 × 2, or only a product rotation's identity letter, pauli=X_), qubit 0 the most significant bit of an index (endian="little": the least). A qubit whose first operation is a reset (R, RX, RY) starts in that state and has no input: a circuit that resets every qubit first gives its state, a 2^n × 1 column (up to 22 qubits), and one that resets some a 2^n × 2^k matrix. Noise channels are skipped (no error drawn), and coordinates and TICK are nothing. At most 2^22 entries (a unitary of 11 qubits).

A circuit with a measurement, a detector or observable, a reset after a qubit's first operation, feedback or a product rotation naming a qubit twice (TPP X0*Y0*X0) is Tsim's ZX tensor of it (tsim.zx.tensorfy), contracted in numpy with every measurement outcome and error bit read as 0: a measurement is the identity on its qubit times 1/sqrt(2) (H 0 then M 0 is [[.5, .5], [.5, -.5]]), a detector or observable is an X spider joined to the measurements it XORs, projecting their parity even (one that XORs none stands alone, a factor of 2: DETECTOR is [[2]], OBSERVABLE_INCLUDE(k) makes the observables 0 to k, so OBSERVABLE_INCLUDE(2) is [[8]]), a reset projects (and prepares), MPP and heralded noise add an output qubit (the first, qubit -2 in Tsim's graph), feedback is the controlled gate. Its qubits are the ZX graph's: the inputs of the qubits first used by a gate, the outputs of every qubit.

Tsim's graph builders refuse what the unitary engine would run (an R_PAULI naming a qubit twice or more than 64, an anti-Hermitian product, a sweep bit): a circuit with Pauli products (SPP, MPP, TPP, R_PAULI, R_XX, ...) or sweep bits has its graph built first, so it raises Tsim's error. That graph is built in Python over the circuit with its loops unrolled, one step per instruction, before the engine runs: it costs seconds where the engine takes milliseconds for tens of thousands of instructions (REPEAT 20000 of SPP X0*Z1 and H 0). Tsim's own to_matrix builds and contracts that graph, and takes far longer. A circuit without products or sweep bits does not build it, and one with an instruction the builders cannot read (a tag the core leaves inert, I[foo(x)] 0) is the engine's matrix, as without products (Tsim's raises its parse error), unless a product rotation names a qubit twice: without a graph that raises Tsim's parse error too. Where a product leaves a qubit out of Tsim's graph (an identity letter's, pauli=X_, or one SPP X0*X0 cancels on), the engine's matrix leaves it out too, and is Tsim's matrix of the circuit without that instruction up to the engine's global phase.

def to_tensor(self, *, endian: str = 'big') -> numpy.ndarray:

to_matrix as a tensor: one index of size 2 per output qubit, then one per input qubit, in the order of endian.

def tcount(self) -> int:

Count the number of T gates in the circuit, as Tsim counts them: the vertices of the circuit's ZX graph with a non-Clifford phase (one for each T, T_DAG and TPP and each rotation by an angle that is not a multiple of 0.5 half-turns, three for a U3 whose angles are none). A coherent error (I_ERROR[R_Z(...)]) is no gate, as in Tsim; stabilizer_qec.Circuit.tcount counts it.

def get_graph(self, *, backend: Optional[str] = None) -> Any:

Construct the ZX graph, as Tsim's get_graph: one lane (a pair of boundary vertices joined by an edge) for each qubit the circuit names, its gates spiders on it, each measurement a Z spider with the phase variable rec[i], each noise channel's error bits spiders with phase variables e{k}, each detector and observable an X spider with the phase variable det[i] or obs[i] joined to the measurements it XORs, and the scalar.

Args: backend: "pyzx_param" for pyzx_param's GraphS (the graph Tsim returns; needs pyzx_param), "own" for this package's tsim.zx.ZXGraph (nothing to install); None (the default) is pyzx_param's if it is installed, else this package's. The two are equal vertex for vertex, edge for edge.

Returns: The ZX graph.

def get_sampling_graph( self, sample_detectors: bool = False, *, backend: Optional[str] = None) -> Any:

Get a ZX graph that can be used to compute probabilities.

This graph will be constructed as follows:

  1. Double the ZX-diagram by composing it with its adjoint.
  2. Connect all rec[i] vertices to their corresponding adjoint rec[i] vertices.
  3. Add outputs: (a) When sampling measurements (i.e. sample_detectors is False), add output nodes for each measurement. Detectors and observables are removed since they are ignored when sampling measurements. (b) When sampling detectors and observables (i.e. sample_detectors is True), add output nodes for each detector and observable. Only one set of detector and observable nodes is kept, i.e., detectors and observables are not composed with their adjoints.

Args: sample_detectors: If True, sample detectors and observables instead of measurements. backend: As get_graph's.

Returns: A ZX graph for sampling.

def diagram( self, type: str = 'timeline-svg', tick: Union[int, range, NoneType] = None, filter_coords: Any = ((),), rows: Optional[int] = None, height: Optional[float] = None, width: Optional[float] = None, zoomable: bool = True, **kwargs: Any) -> Any:

Return a diagram of the circuit, from a variety of options.

Args: type: The type of diagram: "timeline-svg" (the default) or "timeslice-svg", whose tagged gates are labelled (T, R_X(0.1π), TPP[XY], ...), or any of Stim's types ("timeline-text", "detslice-svg", ...), as stabilizer_qec.Circuit.diagram's. Tsim's ZX-graph types ("pyzx", "pyzx-dets", "pyzx-meas") draw the circuit's ZX graph ("pyzx": with bold classical wires) or the sampling graph for measurements or for detectors and observables after pyzx_param's full_reduce(paramSafe=True) (the reduced two need pyzx_param); they draw it with pyzx (or pyzx_param) and return the graph. tick: Required for time slice diagrams. Specifies which TICK instruction, or range of TICK instructions, to slice at. filter_coords: A list of things to include in the diagram (detslice diagrams). rows: In diagrams that have multiple separate pieces, how many rows of pieces. height: Optional height for the rendered diagram in pixels. Only applied to timeline-svg and timeslice-svg diagram types. For timeline-svg, when both height and width are None, the height is automatically determined based on the number of qubits. When only one dimension is given, the other is computed from the SVG aspect ratio. width: Optional width for the rendered diagram in pixels. Only applied to timeline-svg and timeslice-svg diagram types. zoomable: If True (default), wraps SVG diagrams in an interactive container with pan and Ctrl/Cmd+wheel zoom. Only applies to timeline-svg and timeslice-svg diagram types. **kwargs: Additional keyword arguments passed to the underlying diagram renderer.

Returns: An object whose __str__ method returns the diagram (for the SVG types, the raw SVG), and whose _repr_html_ shows it in a notebook at the given size. For the ZX-graph types, the ZX graph that was drawn (Circuit.get_graph's kind).

class CompiledDetectorSampler:

Samples detector and observable outcomes from a quantum circuit, as Tsim's CompiledDetectorSampler: by default each detector's and observable's value, the parity of its measurements (X 0; M 0; DETECTOR rec[-1] reads True), not Stim's flip from a noiseless reference run; use_detector_reference_sample / use_observable_reference_sample give the flips. The shots are this package's samplers': the frame sampler on a Clifford circuit, the stabilizer-rank sampler on a non-Clifford one.

class CompiledStateProbs:

Computes measurement probabilities for a given state, as Tsim's CompiledStateProbs: for each of a batch of noise draws f, the exact probability P(state | f) of an outcome (a measurement record, or with sample_detectors the detectors' and observables' values), from this package's stabilizer-rank engine. Their mean estimates P(state).

With sample_detectors, P(state | f) sums the probabilities of the measurement records whose parities are the state: at most 2^16 of them (a state's records are 2^k, k the measurements no detector or observable pins down, a reset-free circuit's unread measurements among them). A reset of a qubit left in superposition (H 0 then R 0) draws its unread outcome along with the noise, so a draw's probability is given that outcome too; the mean is unchanged.

def get_detector_error_model( circuit: Any, *, allow_non_deterministic_observables: bool = True, decompose_errors: bool = False, flatten_loops: bool = False, allow_gauge_detectors: bool = False, approximate_disjoint_errors: Any = False, ignore_decomposition_failures: bool = False, block_decomposition_from_introducing_remnant_edges: bool = False) -> stabilizer_qec.DetectorErrorModel:

The circuit's detector error model, as Tsim's Circuit.detector_error_model: unlike Stim's, it allows non-deterministic observables (a logical observable a non-Clifford gate leaves random) when allow_non_deterministic_observables is set, by converting the observables into detectors, building the model with allow_gauge_detectors=True, and converting those detectors back into observables; gauge errors on observables alone (error(0.5) L0) are dropped. With decompose_errors that conversion is not supported.

A tsim.Circuit's model is Tsim's: Stim's analysis of Tsim's tagged form, which reads T as S, a rotation or U3 as I and a Pauli-product rotation as SPP (so R 0, H 0, T 0, H 0, M 0 has a random observable, error(0.5) D0 L0); a stabilizer_qec.Circuit's reads I[T] and the rotations as the identity.

WARNING: if the circuit has distance one (an error flips an observable alone), the model is wrong, as Tsim's is.

def stim_to_shorthand(text: str) -> str:

Convert expanded stim annotations back to tsim shorthand.

Rewrites:

  • I[U3(theta=θpi, phi=φpi, lambda=λ*pi)] → U3(θ, φ, λ)
  • I[R_X(theta=α*pi)] / I[R_Y(...)] / I[R_Z(...)] → R_X(α) / R_Y(α) / R_Z(α)
  • SPP[R_PAULI(theta=αpi)] P0P1 → R_PP(α) 0 1 (P ∈ {X, Y, Z})
  • SPP[R_PAULI(theta=αpi)] X0Y1 → R_PAULI(α) X0*Y1
  • SPP[T] → TPP
  • SPP_DAG[T] → TPP_DAG
  • S[T] → T
  • S_DAG[T] → T_DAG

and this package's tagged forms (stabilizer_qec's addition), angles in radians of exp(−iθP) written back in the half-turns they were written in (the shortest decimal number that gives the radians, or the one their further digits give):

  • I[T] / I[T_DAG] → T / T_DAG
  • I[R_X(theta=t)] → R_X(2t/π), and R_Y, R_Z
  • I[U3(theta=a, phi=b, lambda=c)] → U3(a/π, b/π, c/π)
  • I[R_PAULI(theta=±π/8, pauli=XY)] 0 1 → TPP / TPP_DAG X0Y1 (±π/8 as repr writes it; written with further digits it is R_PAULI(±0.25) X0Y1)
  • I[R_PAULI(theta=t, pauli=ZZ)] 0 1 → R_ZZ(2t/π) 0 1; any other product R_PAULI(2t/π) X0*Y1 (II[R_PAULI(...)] as I[R_PAULI(...)], one product over its targets, as the core reads it); one whose letters are all identities (pauli=__) is left out, no operation as the core reads it
  • a product with a ! (a lower-case letter, pauli=xY) or the SPP_DAG of a rotation (its arguments in the other order, pauli=X, theta=t) → Tsim's tagged form, as its shorthand writes it: SPP[R_PAULI(theta=αpi)] !X0Y1, SPP_DAG[R_PAULI(theta=α*pi)] X0, TPP !X0 (see _product_rotation)
  • II[R_XX(theta=t)] 0 1 2 3 → R_XX(2t/π) 0 1 and R_XX(2t/π) 2 3, a line a pair (TPP / TPP_DAG X0X1 at ±π/8 as repr writes it); with an empty argument first, II[R_XX(, theta=-t)] 3 2 1 0, its SPP_DAG, one line as Tsim's inverse of it writes it: SPP_DAG[R_PAULI(theta=2t/πpi)] X3X2 X1X0
